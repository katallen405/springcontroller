--- Per-participant calibration (once, before their 3 randomized-order runs) ---
- eye height, arm length, and finalized target are all read live and don't need a restart for the calibration phase
- changes to physical rig geometry (collision scene) needs a restart

1. Move the arm to the study start location ("Study start location" panel).
2. Seat the participant so their midline is at the pre-set seat location.
3. Adjust the chair height so their elbows are at table height.
4. Measure seated eye height (meter stick, from the table surface) and
   forearm length elbow-to-fingertips (tape laid out on the table), both in
   centimeters.
5. In the "Study workspace calibration" panel: enter the participant ID and
   both measurements in cm, click "Preview workspace center"
6. Push the arm around and/or retarget "workspace_demo" in the "Add /
   remove springs" list below until the location is approved.
7. Click "Finalize study conditions" -- writes
   ~/gen3_study_data/<participant ID>/position.yaml and pose.yaml,
   logs the session to ~/gen3_study_data/measurements.csv, and removes the
   demo spring.

study instructions

- sit at the line on the table

- when you hand the block to the robot, hold it from the sides opposite
  where the robot's gripper is to avoid getting pinched.

- I am going to measure your eye height and arm length to determine
  your ergonomic workspace.  Then we can look at a preview of the workspace
  to figure out if you want any adjustments, and set it for the study.

- There are three conditions which you will see in a random
  order. I'll introduce the way you control the arm in the three
  conditions when we get to them.  In all of them, you will have up to
  2 minutes to decorate two opposite faces of the block.  When you're
  done or time is up, we'll stop and you can answer some questions
  about this way of interacting with the robot arm, and then you will
  get to decorate two other faces of the block in each of the two
  other conditions.  You can stop the test at any time by saying
  "stop", and I may stop the test if I am concerned that something
  unsafe is happening.

Condition KT:

In this condition, you will control the arm using the two buttons on
the wrist of the robot.  The button that is indented allows you to
move the arm by joint, so pushing on a joint will move it.  The button
that is outdented allows you to move the arm with cartesian control -
it will try to move the gripper around in logical x/y/z space.  If you
use both at the same time, it keeps the gripper where it is and moves
the joints in their "latent space" -- moving joints around in the
links that are further back from the gripper that are not constrained
by the current gripper position.  You can use any or all of these
modes at any time to adjust the block position or the rest of the
robot.  If you are not pressing any of the buttons, the robot will be
in "position hold" -- it will not move when you push on it.

Condition position:

In this condition, the torque controller will be active. The torque
controller allows you to push the arm around freely.  We can also
constrain the arm's movement in this condition with virtual springs --
we create them in the control software connecting any part of the arm
to any point in space, with any strength we like.  When this condition
starts, there will be just one spring, connecting the gripper to the
point we identified as the center of your workspace based on your eye
height and arm length and adjusted in the beginning of the study.  You
can add or remove springs at any point during this part of the study
by telling me where you want them on the arm and moving the arm to the
place where you want it to be. To remove a spring, you can tell me
which one you want removed and I will remove it.  We can also "freeze"
the arm into a "position hold" -- in position hold it will not move
when you push on it, and you can tell me if you want to put it back
into the spring controller mode.

Condition pose:

In this condition, the torque controller will be
active.  The torque controller allows you to push the arm around
freely.  We can also constrain the arm's movement in this condition
with virtual springs -- we create them in the control software
connecting any part of the arm to any point in space, with any
strength we like.  When this condition starts, there will be just one
spring, which pulls the gripper towards the point in space we
identified as the center of your workspace based on your eye height
and arm length and adjusted in the beginning of the study, **and**
tries to orient one side of the block towards your face.

In this part of the study, there are two kinds of springs you can use.
One, the pose spring, has both a position and an orientation
component.  You can change the position it pulls to in space, change
where on the block it is attached, and change where it orients to, as
well as the strength of the spring and the length of the "dead zone"
before it starts pulling.  You can also use a position spring, which
just pulls any part of the arm towards a particular point in space.
We can also freeze the arm into a position hold -- in position hold it
will not move when you push on it, and you can tell me if you want to
put it back into the spring controller mode.

All conditions:

You will have 2 minutes to decorate the two sides of
the block.  I will tell you when your time is half finished, and the
time you spend adjusting the robot's position does not count against
your painting time. If you need the robot to let go of the block so
you can change which way the block is facing in the gripper, let me
know and I will help you.
